Zero animations. Every clip all four classes need is already in the folder; what is missing is two characters — a robed caster and something for the Druid. The build order — for whoever wires the next slot

In an idle battler nobody steers the hero, so animation is not feedback on the player's inputs — it is the narration of the fight. Two consequences rank everything here: every skill press must visibly change the hero, because the four runes are the only buttons the game has; and every new pose must pass the thumbnail test, because at 30–62 pixels the silhouette is the whole message. No run-versus-walk, no turns, no strafes, no jumps — nuance below "different outline" is weight.

  1. Hit reactions, game-wide — done, 11 Aug. The highest-value missing animation in the game: damage numbers say you are losing, a body rocking backwards makes you feel it. One recoil cell, held a beat, throttled like the grunt sounds so a surrounded hero does not vibrate. Clips on disk: Getting-Hit · Reaction · Standing React Large From Front. Code: a react state beside walk and death in drawAnimFrame.
  2. The Paladin's death — done, 11 Aug. A bake, not code — extend paladin-walk.png to the same sixteen-cell walk-plus-death layout every foe already uses.
  3. Skill poses for dolls. The actions path already plays a wind-up and a release with the damage on the cut between them; dolls set it to null. Bake two cells per skill and un-null it. This is the entire feedback loop of the four buttons.
  4. The draw at encounter start. The clash sound already marks the moment; the body should too. Withdrawing Sword has been on disk since the first day of the doll work. Needs an encounter-opening state.
  5. A camp idle. The camp panel plays the combat guard, which reads as paranoid around a fire. Idle is on disk.

The identity beat, per class

Beyond the core five, a class earns exactly one animation, where its kit differs — it is listed in each card above as code first. The Warrior's is the battlecry, because his skills are physical and the clip is pure character. The Paladin's is a prayer: one bowed-head cast covers mend and ward both, and his identity is that he barely stops fighting to pray. The Warlock's casts are already his attacks, so his beat is the drain — a cast held mid-phase, arms out, visibly paying the life. The Druid's is the summon; and if he ever shapeshifts, that is two characters cut on the same skeleton behind a cloud, not an animation.

The foes' stack

A foe is read, not befriended, so its animations are threat-grammar: what kind of danger, and when. Fodder needs three clips — walk, one attack, death — because it lives for seconds and an alternating second swing is a hero's luxury. Death is the one place not to economise: it is the most frequent event on the screen and therefore the most-seen animation in the game, and the six-cell collapse is much of why the doll foes already read better than the painted ones. Everything beyond that exists to animate a tell, because the player's only decision is when to spend a cooldown.

  1. The bloater's swell. The exploder detonates with no arming tell — it walks, then pops. "That one is about to blow" is precisely the decision the game asks the player to make, and nothing on screen says it. The highest-value enemy animation missing.
  2. The charger's crouch. The imp's identity is the rush; one beat of gathering before it turns a speed change into a pounce.
  3. The caster's loose. The wraith fires from its idle. The archer has a firing pose and it is why the archer reads; any standing one-hand cast does the same for the wraith.
  4. Boss move poses. Slam, charge, nova and summon ring the ground, light it — and then play the same swing as an ordinary hit. One pose per move, and a roar for the enrage: the growl already sounds while nothing moves.

Not worth a cell: walk variety beyond the one cycle each doll already has, turns, and spawn-ins — the emerge field exists on every monster, but a wave arriving through the road's gates already carries its own entrance.

What makes a clip usable here

  1. It stays in profile. The camera is fixed and side-on. A clip that turns the character to face it has nothing left to draw — two of eight frames of one sword combo came out as a sliver.
  2. In Place is ticked. The game owns position; root motion fights it and the figure moonwalks.
  3. Its middle is as good as its ends. Warrok's punch is correct on the first and last frame and tears in between. Strip every clip before trusting it, not just the pose you want.
  4. The silhouette survives at fifty pixels. Foes are drawn 30–62px tall. Detail below that is weight; outline is everything.
  5. One beat, not a sequence. Sheets are a handful of cells. A three-hit combo spends most of its length on transitions no cell will show.
  6. The weapon is in the download. Mixamo animates around a prop the character file may not contain. A WProp character brings its own; anything else mimes, and a blade has to be built and parented to the hand.

Download settings

Format: FBX Binary. Not “FBX for Unity”, which exports FileVersion 6100 and is refused outright — every file that works here is 7700. The character wants with skin; clips fetched on that character carry their own mesh and drop straight in. A clip taken from another character still works, reduced to its rotations — its positions are in that character's proportions and would drag every joint somewhere it does not belong.

The plan

Written 11 Aug. Five phases, each shippable on its own, in dependency order.

Phase 1 — one particle emitter, many weathers — done, 11 Aug

The 81 Kenney textures are made to be emitted and tinted, and one Canvas 2D emitter serves every atmospheric ask at once, data-driven per biome: { texture, rate, wind, tint, layer }. Ash drifting across the inferno road, dust hanging in the town light, embers rising off braziers, motes in the crypt. The same emitter answers the stomp: a dust ring thrown at the feet plus a cracked-earth stain — the quake shake and the stain system already exist, so a stomp becomes data, not a feature. And it replaces the repurposed footage where footage was the wrong tool: a heal becomes rising green motes rather than a recoloured portal. No new dependency; it draws in the pass the effects already use. Shipped: js/particles.js (pool of 400, weather + named bursts), art/particles.png (8 Kenney cells, 75KB, rebuilt by tools/particle-atlas.mjs), weather on all six biomes, stomp on the quake skill and on slam and nova, motes on heals.

Phase 2 — a placement grammar for props — done, 11 Aug

The windmill question generalised: today one hash scatters everything that is not a landmark, so set dressing has frequency but no rules. Every prop declares a tier instead. Landmark — windmill, gallows, shrine — at most one per stage, placed from the landmark table (landmarkAt already exists). Feature — carts, fences, graves — hash-placed with a minimum gap in tiles so two never crowd one screen. Scatter — grass, stones, bones — the current hash at a per-biome density. Three numbers per prop in world.js, and the answer to "how often should a wind wheel appear" becomes a line of data anyone can tune.

Shipped: tiers live on the prop entry — { name, tier, every, density, edge } — sorted once per biome into a WeakMap, never filtered per tile. slotAt is the whole mechanism: x / every names a window, the hash places the object inside it, on a side, at a distance. Confine that position to the window's middle half. Free to sit anywhere, one object ends a window and the next begins the following one — measured over four thousand tiles the real gap was one tile, which is the crowding the tier exists to prevent. A landmark also clears its own tile now. Verify with node tools/placement-test.mjs: per-window should read 1.00 and min-gap about half a window. Cost 0.045ms/frame over 760 tiles, up from 0.011 — measure this way, not by headless rAF, which reported 93ms of pure throttling.

Where it stands. Blender 4.5 is installed and headless, tools/armour.py builds five outfits and skins them by Data Transfer, tools/doll.html takes ?glb= and ?tier=, and tools/bake-doll.mjs --glb … --tiers 5 bakes a five-row sheet. Proven on both X Bot and the Paladin — the Paladin keeps his own sword and shield — and the five rungs are distinguishable at h=56 on the road. It is wired: the hero is the knight, and his armour changes with what he wears. Verified in the running game — both sheets load, wornTier reads 5 on gold gear, and the figure on the road changes between rungs. The plan said this phase needed no runtime change because tiered: true and row = wornTier − 1 already ship. That is true of the painted warrior and false of the route the game actually draws: DOLL_ART in js/render.js carries rows: 1, row: 0 and merges that row: 0 over the row: wornTier(S.equipped) − 1 that js/game.js:778 already computes. So the wiring is three fields, not none — and it means re-baking the Paladin's shipped sheets with five rows, whose original bake commands are recorded nowhere. Record them this time.

The plates were abandoned for a kit, and that is the bigger decision here. Generated cylinders read at road size and read as grey boxes anywhere larger, so phase 3 now runs on Quaternius' Modular Character Outfits — Fantasy (CC0, bought, in the repo root) through tools/outfit.py. Five outfits replace the body rather than dressing it, which is the tier ladder, and the kit's own readme says to keep only the head. Its rig is Unreal-named and T-posed and maps 1:1 onto mixamorig — but renaming the bones is not enough: the names match and the rolls do not, so the first clip's hip rotation lays every tier flat while the bind pose stands perfectly upright. That is the tell. The fix is to throw the kit's rig away and bind the outfit meshes to the real Mixamo skeleton with the same Data Transfer weights, after which every existing clip drives them untouched. Two measurements were load-bearing: each part file ships a 42-vertex Icosphere helper spanning z −1…1 that inflates the kit's measured height to 2.84 and shrinks every outfit by a third (the symptom is boots weighted to UpLeg), and the body must be chosen by how many bones it reaches, never by vertex count. The head comes from the free Universal Base Characters pack (also in the repo root, also CC0): everything below the neck is deleted and the head goes through the same fit as the outfits, which is the only way it lands on the right shoulders. The hands never needed anything — they are the kit's own, painted by the kit's own material. With the body replaced tier by tier, the doll's ruler had to change too: tools/doll.html now measures the figure from the union of all meshes, hidden tiers included, because that is the only measurement that does not move when a helmet appears.

What phase 3 does not know yet, and it is the lore's doing. LORE.md arrived after this was built and contradicts two choices in it. The tier-5 horns read demonic on a light-ordained guard — they belong to the Nephilim. And the ladder (peasant → ranger → noble → mail → plate) is merchant progression, not an order of light; tier 1 should be an initiate. Bigger: every class has a skill tree with Light, Hybrid and Evil branches, so a hero is a branch and a tier — fifteen outfits per class, not five. The sheets already carry the cheap axis, so a branch is a sheet and a row is still a tier: DOLL_ART becomes three entries, row = wornTier − 1 never changes, and the cost is three bakes per class. Do not bake them yet: nothing in js/ records which branch a hero took, so there is no state to choose a sheet with. Order of work — move the horns off Light tier 5, add the branch to state behind a ?branch= flag, then bake the three branches alongside phase 4 so the palettes are chosen once, against the road.

Two traps the first bakes walked into, both silent. An FBX clip's position tracks are in Mixamo's centimetres and a Blender GLB is in metres, so a character playing his own idle whole is thrown a hundred units into the air and the cell bakes empty — a GLB skin plays rotations-only whoever authored the clip. And a PMREM environment built from a 1-pixel-wide equirect makes every later draw call return GL_INVALID_OPERATION: the canvas is blank while renderer.info still reports 23 calls and 99,922 triangles. Check gl.getError() before suspecting the camera.

Blender runs headless — blender --background --python, the same shape as every baker here — and does the three jobs Mixamo cannot. Armour: kitbash or take CC0 armour kits, skin them to the mixamorig skeleton by weight transfer from the body (minutes per piece in Blender, impossible in the browser), assemble five outfit collections per class, export one GLB. The baker learns GLB and bakes the sheets with five tiered rows — which the runtime already plays: tiered: true and row = wornTier − 1 ship today for the painted warrior. The armoury returns as visible tiers driven by what is worn, the exact compromise the painted classes already made, for zero runtime cost. Clip repair: edit the curves of the sword combo that turns to face the camera so it stays in profile. Authoring: the Druid's staff, and the clips Mixamo lacks — a stomp with real anticipation.

Direction: headless is blender --background --python script.py. Skin armour with the Data Transfer modifier copying weights from the body mesh — automatic weights on a rigid plate is the classic mistake and produces rubber armour. Outfits are collections T1…T5; export one GLB per class; the baker toggles collection visibility by name. Vendor GLTFLoader from the same three r0.185 tree already under tools/vendor. Bake five tiered rows and reuse tiered: true + row = wornTier − 1 — the warrior's painted sheets show the exact shape; do not invent a runtime path. Retargeting rules are unchanged: a clip authored for the character plays whole, a borrowed one plays rotations-only, and some characters have broken bind poses — check clip=none first. One-shots already clamp; keep fh constant per class across every sheet or the hero changes height when he changes state.

Phase 4 — one light, one palette: the direction refresh — the bake half done, 11 Aug

Done: the ground lies on the tile grid (fillIso), the verge has height and pools, ?grid and ?grid=over exist to judge against, the hero carries real weapons per tier, and the bake now takes --key and --posterize — a warmed key and a canvas quantise, exactly where the direction below says they belong. Settings recorded in ART-BRIEF.md §1c, including that posterize 6 is too coarse and 10 is the number. Not done, and it needs new painted art rather than code: the grove, gate and crypt sets to match the town's seven, the four missing camps, and non-directional ground textures to replace the ploughed-looking grain the iso fill exposed.

The dolls read as figures but not yet as this world's figures. The fix is in the bake, where it costs nothing at runtime: key light warmed to the biome palette, a posterize pass so shading steps like paint rather than ramps like plastic, rim light against the dark grounds, and one contact-sheet per character judged at road size on the road — never as a sheet. Then even out the world: grove, gate and crypt painted sets to match the town's seven, and the four missing camps. Every sheet request goes through ART-BRIEF.md rules that already exist; the brief gains a section for doll bake settings so the look is reproducible.

Phase 5 — deferred: the live hero — recommend closing it unbuilt

The condition for deciding has been met: phase 3 has been seen on the road, and it answers the question phase 5 was asked to answer. Five tiers of armour and five weapons read at 56 pixels, driven by row = wornTier − 1 with no runtime 3D at all. What phase 5 would add over that is *per-slot* truth — a gold gauntlet on a leather chest — and the kit that arrived makes even that reachable in the bake, since its parts are modular per slot: a handful of mixed sets is a `TIERS` edit, not a renderer. Against which phase 5 costs the project its first runtime 3D dependency and has to thread a WebGL canvas through drawDepthPass as a sprite. Recommend it stays unbuilt until something is actually blocked by sheets.

True per-slot mix-and-match — a gold gauntlet on a leather chest, visible — means rendering the hero from meshes at runtime instead of from sheets. It is one small WebGL canvas composited at the hero's depth, and it is deliberately last: phase 3 buys visible progression for nothing, and this buys per-slot truth at the cost of the first runtime 3D dependency in the project. Decide only after phase 3 has been seen on the road; it may not be wanted.

Direction, if it is ever built: the hard constraint is drawDepthPass — sprites interleave by depth, and a WebGL canvas composites only as a whole. So the hero renders alone to a small offscreen WebGL canvas and that canvas is drawn as his sprite, at his slot in the sorted pass. Never a full-scene overlay. Lazy dynamic import behind a URL flag; the sheets stay the default until this has been judged on the road.

Traps — read before touching anything

  1. The Bash shell's cwd drifts back to the parent folder between calls. cd explicitly in every command, or edits silently hit the unrelated Deep Dig project sitting beside this one.
  2. Grep misses computed access. Audio.sfx[cond ? 'a' : 'b']() is how this codebase calls its most important sounds, and heroStats is recomputed by game.js and ui.js — override at the source, never at a call site. This produced two confidently wrong claims in one session.
  3. A slot rule that may place its object anywhere in its window guarantees nothing: neighbours meet at the boundary and the real minimum gap is one tile. Confine to the middle half, and measure — tools/placement-test.mjs is the pattern for proving any placement claim.
  4. Binaries from swiftc are SIGKILLed on launch, no output, until codesign -s -.
  5. afconvert -d aac, never aac@44100 — the rate pin refuses some 48k sources with a useless error.
  6. new URL(...).pathname keeps %20; use fileURLToPath for anything under a path with spaces.
  7. The Browser pane's navigate strips query strings — reach dev hooks with location.href from inside the page.
  8. ?fresh escapes the saved boss-loop trap; serve.js is deliberately no-store — do not add caching back.
  9. Atlas.tinted caches one canvas per colour string. Tint with fixed palette strings; a colour built per frame is a memory leak shaped like a rainbow.
  10. The animation mixer's default loop wraps at exactly t = duration — doll.html clamps one-shots for this; keep it, or every death bakes a corpse that stands back up in its last cell.

How these were judged

Forged means seen working, not merely written — most of stratum IV was screenshotted in a running fight. Rough is almost always coverage rather than quality: the system works and the content behind it is uneven, which is why the biomes, the foes and the camps all land there together.

Two parts are cast aside and that is not a defect. The jointed rig and the painted class sheets both work; they were beaten by something that reads better and cannot do what they do. The armoury is the real cost of that trade, and it is the largest open question in the game — larger than any single row above.
